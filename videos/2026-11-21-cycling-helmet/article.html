It is one of the most common questions we get, usually from a parent who already owns a bike helmet and is wondering whether they need to buy anything else. The short answer is that a certified cycling helmet is almost always better than no helmet, and in Europe the same standard covers both sports. But there are real differences, and one of them matters a lot for beginners.

The standard already covers both

EN1078 is the European standard for helmets, and its scope is explicit: helmets for cyclists and for users of skateboards and roller skates. One standard, three activities. So a helmet carrying a CE EN1078 mark has been tested for skating use, whether the box has a bicycle on it or a skateboard.

That is the part most people miss. The question is not really "bike helmet or skate helmet" — it is whether the helmet in your hands has been tested to any standard at all. A great many cheap helmets sold for skating carry no stated certification. An EN1078 cycling helmet from a reputable brand is a better choice than an uncertified helmet sold as a skating helmet.

Where a cycling helmet genuinely falls short

Three practical differences are worth knowing.

Coverage at the back of the head

This is the important one. Road cycling helmets are shaped around the risk of going over the bars, so they extend forward and sit higher at the back. Skate-style helmets come further down over the occipital bone.

Beginner skaters fall backwards. Your feet slide out in front of you and the back of your head is the first thing to reach the ground. That is precisely the area a road helmet covers least. If you are learning, or buying for a child who is learning, the extra rear coverage is not a detail.

Repeated impacts

Most EN1078 helmets, cycling and skating alike, use EPS foam that crushes once to absorb energy and does not recover. They are single-impact. Some skate-style helmets are built to a different standard, such as the American ASTM F1492, which tests for multiple impacts — relevant if you are learning tricks and going down repeatedly.

Either way: replace any helmet after a significant impact, even if the shell looks perfect. The damage is in the liner, and you cannot see it.

Ventilation, weight and shape

Road cycling helmets are usually well ventilated and light, which is why they transfer well to skating. Classic skate-style helmets with a hard shell and few vents are hot — and in Indian conditions, a hot helmet is a helmet that stays in the bag.

What about speed skating?

For inline speed and road skating, a light, well ventilated helmet is the right tool, and the requirements look much more like cycling than skateboarding. You are moving fast in a straight line, you want airflow, and you want the lowest weight you can get on your neck over a long session. This is why our own skating helmets are closer in shape to a road cycling lid than to a skate park helmet: 250 grams, open vent channels, CE EN1078 certified.

Short track ice racing is the exception. Those helmets are a compact, low profile shell designed to sit under a race hood, with the impact patterns of close pack racing in mind. A cycling helmet is not a substitute there.

So should you buy a skating helmet?

Be honest about which skater you are.

  • Learning to skate, or buying for a child: get a skate-style helmet with proper rear coverage. Backward falls are the whole reason.
  • Recreational skating, you already own a certified cycling helmet: wear it. It is tested to the right standard and it is on your head, which is most of the battle.
  • Inline speed or road skating: prioritise weight, ventilation and a certified shell. A good road cycling helmet works; a purpose built skating helmet does the same job with a fit meant for the position you skate in.
  • Short track ice: buy the short track helmet.

How any helmet should fit

A helmet that fits badly is doing a fraction of its job. Measure around your head about two centimetres above the eyebrows and buy to that number. The helmet should sit level, roughly two finger widths above your eyebrows, snug with no pressure points, and it should not shift when you shake your head before you tighten anything. Set the chin strap so two fingers fit between strap and chin.

The honest summary

A certified cycling helmet is a legitimate skating helmet — the standard says so. It is weaker at the back of the head, which matters most for the people falling most. If you already own one, wear it today. If you are buying for a beginner, buy for the backward fall.
