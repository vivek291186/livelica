Train most days, or want the most speed for your money, and a precision steel set such as Red or Super is the better buy and leaves you budget for wheels.

Why bearings matter

Every push you make has to travel through your bearings before it reaches the ground. Lower friction means more roll for each stride, less energy wasted as heat, and a bearing that stays fast deeper into a long race. Eight bearings sit inside a single four wheel skate and sixteen in a pair. How they perform comes down to two things only: how the bearing was built, and how well you look after it.

The anatomy of a skate bearing

A bearing is made of an inner race and an outer race, a set of balls that roll between them, a cage that keeps the balls evenly spaced, and seals that keep dirt and water out.

A 608 skate bearing in cross section.

Ceramic versus steel

Ceramic balls are lighter and harder than steel balls. Steel bearings, when they are made to a high standard, still roll beautifully and cost less.

Hybrid ceramic or full ceramic: what you are actually buying

When a skate bearing is sold as ceramic, it nearly always means hybrid ceramic, which is ceramic balls running in steel races.

Grey rings are steel, blue rings are ceramic.

Maintenance: the free speed most skaters ignore

Clean, well oiled bearings roll faster and last far longer than neglected ones.
